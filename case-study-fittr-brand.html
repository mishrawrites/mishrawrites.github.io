Case Studies / FITTR

Making fitness fun, approachable and easy

How I repositioned FITTR from a six-pack brand to preventive health and wellbeing for everyone: the audience, the promise, the look and voice, and the brand film that carried them.

Summary

FITTR had the product and the proof: more than 200,000 real transformations. People saw it as a brand for gym enthusiasts chasing six-packs. I repositioned it as a holistic, preventive health and wellbeing brand for every kind of person, and took that to people who had never heard of FITTR. It ran from audience segmentation through positioning, tonality and a rebrand, to the brand film Fitness Made Forever and a card game that taught nutrition through play.

Where FITTR stood

I benchmarked FITTR against the two biggest fitness apps in India. FITTR trailed on reach and led on community, by a distance.

The brief sized the opportunity: India's fitness app category stood at nearly 100M users and grew by up to 156% during the pandemic, while FITTR's discovered base was about 5M. The job was to reach people whose earlier attempts had not worked, and get them to install FITTR.

Who we spoke to

I mapped India's fitness audience into five behaviour types, because attitude to fitness predicted behaviour better than age did.

The Indifferent Procrastinator

“I can't care enough”

The Tender Surrenderer

“The gym and diet are my personal hell”

The Sometimer

“Got to go through penance once in a while”

The Active (Imperfect) Fitter

“Fitness is a routine”

The Perfection Striver

“My physique is my life goal”

Communication focused on the Sometimer and the Active (Imperfect) Fitter: 30 to 45, SEC A+ to A, Tier 1 and 2 cities, working professionals short on time who enjoy life's indulgences and have tried, and stalled, before.

The tension and the promise

The consumer tension

Is being the best me sustainable?

I explored seven insight routes, from procrastination to relationship wellness, and the brief landed on this one. People believed they could transform once, and doubted they could live that way for life. The promise answered that doubt directly.

Single-minded proposition

The brand gives me a chance to re-envision fitness as a lifelong transformative experience.

Reason to believe

200,000+ real people have transformed their lives with FITTR, inside a community 650,000 strong.

The voice and the look

The persona I wrote was the friendly sage: inspiring, guiding, relatable, humorous and empowering, with myth-busting science underneath.

The rebrand moodboard, July 2021

This is where the repositioning became visible. The rebrand brief was to make working out fun, approachable, easy and for anyone and everyone. The brand world was human, accessible, real and relatable, and all about small victories. The F of FITTR became a stamp of transformation on before-and-after images, warm and relatable imagery led the look, and the journey mattered more than the end result.

The brand film: Fitness Made Forever

A mass launch for people with low familiarity and zero awareness of FITTR. I wrote the creative brief with three business objectives: grow revenue by up to 10% of the current run rate, grow market share at or above the category for the chosen audience, and grow the non-paying subscriber base by 20%. The brief asked for relatable slice-of-life situations and a light, humorous tone, with an authoritative takeaway.

The Game of Macros

A trump-card game with Uno-style twists, built to make nutrition second nature to anyone who played it a few times. Each card is a food or dish with its macros. Switch cards change what wins a round: Muscle-Up flips the game to the highest calories, Lean Out to the lowest, and Hydrate to water content. Water is the ace and beats every card, a nod to the one nutrient you can't go days without.

The brand job was to take FITTR's philosophy to a wider audience offline, through play. I designed the rollout to start with FITTR's coaches, as part of a starter kit with new coach subscriptions, then move to the FITTR shop and e-commerce. The game launched.
