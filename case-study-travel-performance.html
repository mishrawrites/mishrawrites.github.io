Case Studies / Canada-based travel brand

Building a multi-million-dollar performance engine for a premium travel brand

A brand that sold through partners, booking agents and organic demand needed a paid engine it could plan around. Over 19 months I built one that delivered 6,000 sales qualified leads (SQLs) a quarter in both peak seasons.

What SQL means here

SQL means sales qualified lead: an enquiry that the client's sales team reviewed and accepted as qualified. Every lead figure on this page is an SQL.

The problem

The brand sells premium, fully planned trips with an average value of about $15,400. Its demand came from partners, booking agents and organic reach. No structured paid system existed.

The business is seasonal. The peak booking season, December to April, brought roughly 55% to 60% of annual revenue in five months. The other seven months, May to November, brought the remaining 40% to 45%. A brand with that shape needs a source of qualified demand that it can plan, test and scale.

The approach

I treated paid media as a system with a target. The target was 6,000 SQLs per quarter in the peak season. The method was to build and test one step at a time, measure what came out the other end as SQLs and booking value, and let those two numbers guide every decision.

  • I met the CEO and the person who ran the brand's organic channel to set direction.
  • I planned each campaign as a whole before any ad went live.
  • I wrote the copy for every ad myself and worked with the designers to improve the creative.
  • I presented the final creative and copy to the CEO for approval, and the client billed the media budget each month.
  • I coordinated the consultants, the freelancers and one in-house coordinator, and supervised each campaign after launch.

The direction

The buyers were mostly couples and older travellers in six markets: Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Germany and the Middle East. They wanted a bucket-list journey, planned for them.

I organised the work around six creative themes: Testimonials, Bucket lists, Experience, Offers, Events and FOMO. Meta was the primary channel, search the secondary one and display the third. LinkedIn, X, Reddit and affiliates formed a smaller fourth group that I tested.

What the tests showed

  • Experience-led creative brought the most SQLs, 25% of the peak total, and the highest booking value at about $25,600.
  • Testimonials and Bucket lists each brought 20% of peak SQLs.
  • FOMO creative brought the fewest SQLs, 8% of the peak total, at the lowest booking value, because its bookings were mostly single travellers instead of couples or families. It was also the steadiest theme, holding about 94% of its SQLs in the low season.
  • Events creative fell hardest out of season, down about 71%.

Scaling the engine

Once the themes and channels were proven, I scaled the system through the seasons. At peak it delivered about 2,000 SQLs a month, and in the low season about 1,000.

The strongest result

The client set up an in-house team once the system was established, and that team took it over. I count that as the strongest result, because it shows the system worked well enough to keep.

The scope of these numbers

What the numbers cover

Ad cost per SQL covers media spend only. The sales follow-up and booking engines that run after an enquiry sit with the client's sales team. Bookings, revenue and margin stay with the client, so this page reports the metric I managed, which is SQLs.

What I carried forward

  • Test one variable at a time, and write down what each test taught.
  • Judge creative by SQLs and booking value, so volume never hides quality.
  • Match each theme to the season it works in.
  • Build the system so the client's own team can run it.

How the numbers work

  • All campaign data stayed in the client's systems after the engagement. I reconstructed the figures from my own records and from the recollection of the consultants who worked on the account, so they are approximate and rounded.
  • The 29,000 SQL total is derived from the monthly averages, 2,000 for each of 10 peak months and 1,000 for each of 9 low months.
  • Amounts are converted from Canadian dollars at about 0.73 US dollars. Booking values are rounded up and costs are rounded down.
  • Each section states its scope.
