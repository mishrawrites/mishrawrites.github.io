A follow-along for building a site like this one, and what it actually costs

First of all: do you even need a website?

  • Why?
  • What will the website do?
  • What passion do you have that can be fulfilled by a website?
  • Can your short-term goals be achieved without one?

If the answers you have for the questions above make sense to you, follow along.

I didn't want to build a site. I needed a portfolio, and I had a damn good portfolio on Canva. I still wonder if this website will ever be as sharp and impactful as that Canva portfolio was. Sometimes I feel I traded impact for ground to cover.

It's your call. If you're convinced you want to build one, here's what I did.

Start with everything you've got

I started by collating all the information I had from years of work, the most important piece being the portfolio presentation itself. Then came the harder part: sifting through a lot more information to find what actually deserved a place on the website.

I had at least 100 pages of MS Word, roughly 8,000 words of raw content. Yes, it feels like a lot. More is better here.

Find your voice

Not a voice recording. A simple statement that defines your personality when you talk to someone. It matters that you look and sound the same way. Pen it down.

Find your visual identity

Same idea as voice, applied to colors, shapes, and visual identity. The colors that look good to you may not be the colors that represent you. They might be, but it's not always the case. Find your own color. Find your own style.

Reality-check it with people

Discuss with friends, colleagues, and mentors whether your style, tone, color, and narrative actually resonate with what they think about you. If needed, meet strangers and talk to them for a few minutes, then ask open-ended questions. If they need clues, give them some, and collect the information.

Write your own brand bible

After you've nailed all of this down, make a document. A bible where you're Jesus. A Gita where you're Krishna.

Then refine it. Use AI if you have to, but keep reminding it of your core belief system and don't let it drift. AI systems tend to overpower your philosophy and quietly build something else entirely, and you won't even notice it happening.

It's just as important to define your guardrails, who you're not. Once you've made your own brand guidelines, read them a few times and confirm: is this actually you? If yes, you're halfway there. Honestly, almost there.

At this point you have:

  1. A purpose statement, why you need a website
  2. A few testimonials from friends, colleagues, and mentors
  3. A ton of data on your work, projects, achievements, skills
  4. Your own brand guidelines

Bring in the AI

Tell your AI about your intentions, whether that's Claude or Codex, and let it actually understand the project before it writes anything.

Validate, validate, validate

Keep validating it against what could improve. Stress-test it against what you see around the world. It may take a few days before you're happy with it. Don't rush to deploy. Sleep over it, not for too long, a couple of days is fine, not a couple of weeks. Some ideas strike a bit late.

Deployment

Once your HTML site is ready, here's how I did it.

The domain name idea had been sitting in my mind for a while, so it was easy for me. Find something that resonates with you, something you feel good about. You can buy it from anywhere. I bought mine from Namecheap, and it cost me INR 667 including taxes and everything, for the first year. Buy only the domain name. Nothing else.

Building on GitHub:

  1. Create a repo named exactly yourusername.github.io. This alone turns on GitHub Pages for you, no separate setup needed.
  2. Click "Add file" → "Upload files" in the repo, and drag in your HTML files and any images.
  3. Scroll down, add a commit message, and click "Commit changes" directly to the main branch.
  4. Add a CNAME file with just your domain name in it, so GitHub knows to serve your custom domain instead of the default *.github.io one.
  5. Point your domain's DNS at GitHub (A records for the root domain, a CNAME record for www), then wait for it to propagate.
  6. Every time you want to update the site, repeat step 2 and 3 with just the files that changed.

A personalized email, without paying for one:

I already owned mishrawrites.com, purchased for INR 667/year, that's for the domain, not the email. The email itself cost nothing.

Here's how it works: gaurav@mishrawrites.com isn't a real mailbox. It's a routing rule sitting on top of my existing Gmail. Anyone who emails it gets silently forwarded to my actual Gmail address. When I reply, it goes out showing gaurav@mishrawrites.com. Gmail does all the actual work, for free.

  1. Added mishrawrites.com to Cloudflare (free account)
  2. Switched nameservers on Namecheap from their default to Cloudflare's two nameservers
  3. Inside Cloudflare, Email → Email Routing, created the rule: gaurav@mishrawrites.com → forward to my Gmail address
  4. Deleted the old Namecheap MX records and the SPF TXT record that were blocking activation
  5. Cloudflare added its own MX records automatically, Email Routing went active
  6. In Gmail, Settings → Accounts and Import → Send mail as, added gaurav@mishrawrites.com via SMTP with an App Password
  7. Set gaurav@mishrawrites.com as the default sender

Every email in and out now carries gaurav@mishrawrites.com. Gmail stays the inbox. Zero extra cost, zero second account to manage.

Voilà. You have a site of your own. A blog too, included, where people can even comment. You could build a community or a newsletter on top of this, if you wanted to take it further.

So, you built a website?

Don't worry about anyone asking you that. Answer something to yourself first: did you know how to do all this before? Have you ever done this before?

If the answers were no, then it was worth everything.

Just have fun. Keep learning and keep exploring.
