Repositioning a payroll brand for bigger logos

Taking Paybooks from an India-first, referral-fed payroll brand to a people management portfolio inside TransPerfect.

Summary

Paybooks had a product its clients stayed with for years and a business that grew mostly on referrals. I rebuilt the brand on what larger clients told us they valued, moved the ICP upmarket, repriced for scale and added outbound channels that turned ROI positive. Then I took the brand into a global portfolio after the TransPerfect acquisition.

Starting point, 2023

An India-first payroll brand with a client base heavy on micro-businesses, an average MRR of $37 per new client, and pricing that lacked commercial conviction. Growth came mostly from referrals. Outbound, inorganic GTM was still to be built, and so was the measurement of progressive growth.

The research

I ran 70 interviews with HR leaders, payroll heads, founders and finance heads: 30 existing clients and 40 ICP companies we had yet to sign, reached through HR communities and my own network. Alongside them ran an NPS programme across 600 clients.

70ICP interviews, 30 clients and 40 non-clients
600clients in the NPS programme
33.4NPS for managed payroll, 51 scored responses
22.5NPS for DIY payroll software, 138 scored responses
  • Across both products, the most repeated request was a dedicated, consistent point of contact.
  • On DIY payroll software, larger clients were the most satisfied, especially on value for money. Smaller clients across both products raised more service and pricing issues.
  • Payroll accuracy and responsiveness scored highest, with average ratings of 4.55 and 4.59 out of 5 for managed payroll.
In one line

Larger clients valued compliance certainty and a single point of accountability above price.

The core decision

We traded smaller accounts for bigger logos. The reworked pricing lowered the per-employee-per-month cost at scale, which made Paybooks a clear fit for mid and large companies and a harder fit for micro and small ones. I made that trade knowingly, and the MRR and lifespan numbers below are what it bought.

What I did, in order

  1. Q2 2023

    Research

    70 interviews and the NPS programme above, run before the TransPerfect acquisition.

  2. Q3 2023

    Rebrand

    A full rebrand rooted in that research. I rebuilt the collateral ecosystem and introduced WhatsApp and LinkedIn as outreach channels. Both turned ROI positive, and India CAC fell from $680 to $545.

  3. Q2 2025

    Global People Solution and EOR launch

    I did the groundwork for a brand extension spanning Employer of Record, Global HCM and Multi-country Payroll across 43 countries: naming, brand architecture, positioning, plans and packaging, and GTM. I onboarded the first two clients myself. Most recently I added GCC as a Service to the same brand extension.

  4. Q4 2025

    Pricing overhaul

    Upmarket pricing and packaging for the India business, with revised plans, page copy and sales policy. Across two years, average MRR per new India client rose from $37 to $143.

  5. Q2 2026

    Legacy client migration

    I built the business case and segmented 290 legacy India clients into cohorts by plan age, usage and MRR, each with its own messaging. The Customer Success team ran the migration under my supervision, and I stepped into the roughly 10% of negotiations that turned complex. It added $1M in ARR.

Beyond the plan

A benefit the plan never targeted

The upmarket shift was built for revenue per client. It also made clients stay longer.

Looking back

In my words

I'd repeat the research and the organic optimization. I'd change the brand system to align more closely with TransPerfect's global equity, once the India-only products and services are sunset and everything is positioned as truly global, with Indian companies using the same global products.
