FITTR Ambassador Program

How 700 fitness coaches became FITTR's organic acquisition channel, and its most visible experts.

Summary

FITTR's coaches were already on social media, and most had small audiences. I conceived and led a program that gave coaches the skills and production support to grow those audiences, be recognized as experts in their own right, and turn that recognition into a client pipeline they owned. In one quarter, enrollments driven directly by coaches grew 35% over the previous year, and FITTR's own channels grew alongside them.

The challenge

FITTR's roster of about 700 coaches kept a presence on Facebook and Instagram as a requirement, and many were also active on LinkedIn, YouTube, Quora, Medium, Reddit and Twitter.

Stated objective

Help coaches build an organic, engaged following on social media, and be recognized as experts by the people who followed them.

Underlying objective

Make coaches self-sufficient at attracting clients, so FITTR's acquisition funnel grows while marketing spend holds steady.

A coach's audience was also their client pipeline. The bigger and more trusted the following, the more directly it fed their business.

What the data showed

  • Coaches who were active on social media said they invested time, money, or both in their presence.
  • The top 3% of active coaches earned more than 3X the average of the rest of the pool.
  • More than 30% of the least active coaches held a second job, which limited their time.
  • Coaches who posted rarely pointed to gaps in public speaking, presentability, design and video editing.
In one line

Posting frequency followed skills and available time. Closing those two gaps would move the numbers, and the 3X earnings difference gave coaches a reason they could see in their own income.

The program

  1. 3 to 6 months

    Skills training during apprenticeship

    Coaches trained on design, video editing, public speaking, soft skills and communication, and camera and microphone skills. I invited guest coaches and mentors from outside FITTR, and each ran between 2 and 8 sessions.

  2. Ongoing, $13 a month

    The enablement squad

    I built a squad of content creators by hiring skilled members of FITTR's own community who wanted to work with the company. The squad supported coaches with strategy, post creation, research and ideation, video editing, account management, and follower and engagement growth. The nominal fee made sure coaches took the program seriously and set a real expectation on both sides.

  3. 3 months, Instagram

    The FITTR Ambassador competition

    My social media team and I ran an internal competition on who could grow fastest with real organic growth on Instagram. I chose Instagram for its long-term potential as a client-acquisition channel.

Attribution

Every coach received a dedicated landing page and a unique app download link for their social channels, so each app download traced back to the coach who drove it.

Impact

The program's direct aim was to give coaches a pipeline of their own and the recognition of experts in their own right. That happened. Nearly half the roster joined the enablement squad within three months, follower growth across coaches passed 3.5 times its earlier pace, and enrollments driven directly by coaches grew 35% year on year. Coaches who once had a few hundred followers built audiences that trusted them enough to become clients, a kind of growth built on trust the coaches owned.

Beyond the plan

The program was built for the coaches, and the central brand gained too. FITTR's own channels grew followers 200%+ month on month, and its community produced almost 600% more content over three months: before-and-after photos, healthy recipes and training videos. FITTR already relied on user-generated content in its app and on social media, with more than 60% of its content coming from users then and now. In fitness and wellness, advocacy and results from real users carry the most weight, so this fed a pipeline the brand already depended on.

My role

I conceived the program and wrote the detailed plan. I presented it, then scoped the project timeline and budget. With my social media team, I executed it and ran the competition phase. I managed the people involved, including the guest coaches and mentors, and built the support engine by hiring community members who had the right skills and wanted to work with FITTR.

This program sat inside a bigger three-year story at FITTR. Read how the community grew from 200K to 800K+.

In my words

I'd repeat the conversations with coaches. Talking to them is how I learned to help them become stronger and more confident on social media. In the next version, I'd bring the full roster into enablement, using wins from the first season to bring more coaches on board, with 80% as the goal. I'd also add more platforms, so coaches reach their audience on more than one and depend less on any single platform. The lesson I took forward: recognition drives participation, and a program works best when the people in it can see their own results.
